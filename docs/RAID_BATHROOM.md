# RAID: the guardhouse bathroom, modelled in 3D

CUSTOM. The RAID arena level `portdata/USA/Data/raid1.lvl` is now the
guardhouse bathroom (Stage 4, `ROOM4070`) built as a real 3D room: boxes with
tiled materials, low-poly models for the furniture, real lighting, and a
working mirror. The previous arena is kept as `raid1_arena.lvl`.

The room's two backgrounds (`RC4070` / `RC4071`) are its **reference**, not its
skin. An earlier attempt projected the pictures onto boxes (`bgsrc`, flag 8);
from the shoulder camera that smeared and ghosted wherever the boxes were not
exactly the furniture, and was abandoned. The projection code is still there
and still works for a level that wants it.

Status (2026-10-02): played in game, solo and co-op on loopback. The user's
verdict on the last round: "much better"; the mirror works, both players are
reflected.

## Measured, not guessed

Every size and place in the level was found by casting rays through the room's
own two cameras (the RDT camera records, which project the pictures to the
pixel) onto known planes - the floor, a wall, a rim height - and, where two
pictures see the same thing, by triangulating between them. Picking points is
done on the RE-ENHANCE 1280x960 repaint of camera 0 and on the x4 upscale of
camera 1; the scratch scripts that did it are not tracked, but the method is:

1. Grid the picture, read the point.
2. `hit(camera, px, py, axis, value)`: the ray through that pixel, met with the
   plane `axis = value`.
3. Overlay the level's wireframe (boxes and every `mesh`) back onto both
   pictures and compare. Everything in the level sits on its counterpart.

Findings that changed the room: the door is 1180 x 3195 (it had been guessed
1450 x 2392, which is why its texture came out skewed); the wall has skirting
to 470, stained green plaster to 1150 and a deep rail to 1915; the bath is
2930 x 1600 with its rim at 1080, the basin's rim is at 1250, the cistern is a
low wide box behind the pan, and the shelf under the mirror really is at 2107.

## What it is made of

- **Materials** (`tbox`, `mesh ... <tex> <tile>`): `Data/raidtex/t<n>.bin`,
  'RTEX' + w + h + RGBA, tiled by world position with a LINEAR_WRAP sampler.
  - cut out of the pictures and straightened by
    `tools/build_raid_textures.py --pack <RE-ENHANCE>` (Capcom's pixels: NOT
    tracked): t1 boards, t2 floor tiles, t3 enamel, t4 dark wood, t5 the door,
    t6 the rack's towel, t10 the bath mat, t15 the mat by the pan, t16 green
    plaster, t17 skirting, t18 the bath's outside, t19 the cistern, t20 the
    pan's lid, t21 the bath towel. Each is extracted along the world axes the
    arena lays that face by, from the face's low corner, so a one-off picture
    (the door, a mat) lands on its box exactly.
  - painted from noise by `tools/build_raid_materials.py` (ours, tracked in
    `portdata`): t7 brass, t9 towel cloth, t11 porcelain, t13 iron, t14 soap,
    t23 nickel, t24 the bottle.
- **Models** (`mesh`): `Data/raidmesh/m<id>.obj`, generated by
  `tools/build_raid_meshes.py` (ours, tracked). Game units, Y negative up,
  wound outward. The bath (outside and inside are separate meshes, so the
  enamel and the dark shell take different materials), pan, lid, basin, the
  shelf under the mirror and its bottle, the trough and tray over the bath,
  the mixer with its short spout, the hand shower and its hose, the towel
  draped over the rim, the rack and its towel, soap, sponge, cistern brass,
  paper holder, door knob, mirror frame.
- **Light.** The level's own `ambient` and `light` lines, per vertex with a
  normal (`RaShadeLit`), smooth normals on the models. One light over the mat
  and the bath where the pictures have their pool of light, one over the basin
  corner, one dim fill.

## The mirror

`mirror <axis> <plane> <min> <max> <ytop> <ybot>` in the level.

- **The characters** are reflected by the game's own code. Story rooms with a
  mirror arm it with SCD opcode 0x0F (`cmd_mirror_set`): two flag bits, the
  plane and its extent, and a second joint array per body (`entity +0xAC`) the
  reflection is drawn from. `update_player_anim` and `update_entities` then
  submit each body again through the room camera reflected about the plane
  (`entity_draw_mirror_reflection`). `RaidMirror_Arm` (RaidLevel.cpp) does what
  the opcode does, once per room entry, for both players and every enemy -
  after every body exists. A body whose animation table does not look like one
  the game loads (the co-op player-zombie, assembled by the port) gets no copy
  and is simply not reflected; the reads through `+0xAC` that hit effects make
  on any entity are guarded for such a body. The copies are paid out of the
  load arena, so the bill is added up first and checked against
  `g_DataBuffer`.
- **The room** is reflected by the arena (`RaDrawMirror`): every box and model
  again through the reflected eye, clipped to the room side of the glass. That
  projection is the mirror image, with depths that are the true path length
  through the glass, so it lands behind the wall and shows only through the
  hole the level leaves for the glass (the high-X wall is four `tbox` pieces,
  flag 64 so their boards still line up).
- **The glass**: a faint blue-grey film, blended and not depth-writing, drawn
  after the model pass (`RaidArena_DrawLate`) so it tints the reflected
  characters as well as the reflected room.

A character is reflected only when the line from the camera to its reflection
crosses the glass - stand in front of it to see yourself.

## Known gaps

- A co-op **client** shows the reflected room but no reflected characters: a
  client runs neither `update_player_anim` nor `update_entities`, which is
  where the mirror pass lives.
- No shadows. The pictures have strong ones (the towel's in the bath).
- The shelf under the mirror is narrower than the pictures (300 deep against
  575) on purpose, so it does not hide the basin's taps from the shoulder
  camera.
- The towel rack's towel is a drape, not the pictures' exact folds.
